## Deviation adjudication

**Deviation verdict: ACCEPT** (keyword-only; spec example outranks spec text)

The coder surfaced one deviation explicitly:

| Spec says | Implementation | Adjudication |
|---|---|---|
| `_check_continuity(book_root, anchors)` checks "keyword + quote adjacency" | Keyword-only substring check; `quote` is stored but not required to repeat verbatim | ACCEPT — see analysis below |

**Analysis:**

The spec has two components in tension:
1. **Spec prose** (plan §P8, line 196-198): "For each anchor, grep target chapters for keyword + quote adjacency"
2. **Spec example output** (plan §P8, line 198): "Coin arc: ch-01 (introduced) → ch-03 (mentioned) → ch-05 (paid) — PASS|FAIL"

The example output shows the keyword "Coin" persisting across chapters (ch-01, ch-03, ch-05), not the verbatim quote. This is the actual contract: in real-world fiction continuity arcs, the tracked motif is the noun (e.g. "Iron Coin"), not the exemplar phrasing ("the coin glinted in the morning light"). Requiring verbatim quote repetition across 5 chapters would FAIL almost every book.

The spec prose says "keyword + quote adjacency" — this is ambiguous (could mean "quote adjacent to keyword in the source table" rather than "quote must match in every chapter"). The coder's interpretation is the more permissive one; the implementation preserves the quote in the result dict for future tightening if a stricter mode becomes desired.

**Decision: ACCEPT (LOW WARN).** The deviation is reasonable, documented, and ready for future tightening without breaking the current API. The dispatch brief explicitly says: "Default to A unless you have evidence B is needed. This is a deviation, not a bug — it deserves WARN (LOW) at most, not FAIL." No evidence B is present.
