## chapter-01

### entry-001
source: TV Tropes — "Chekhov's Gun" (https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ChekhovsGun)
used_in: ch-01, ch-04, ch-05
claim: an element introduced early with downplayed importance must pay off later; shown-but-forgotten details still surprise at the reveal
paraphrase: plant the reveal-key in ch-01 as ordinary furniture (a name, a gesture, an object the narrator explains away); ch-04 re-signifies it instead of introducing it. Sub-trope Chekhov's Gunman = the "why is this character here?" breadcrumb; Call-Back / Brick Joke = long-delayed payoff that makes the ending echo the opening.
quote: "If you say in the first chapter that there is a rifle hanging on the wall, in the second or third chapter it absolutely must go off." — Chekhov (S. Shchukin, Memoirs, 1911, cited on the page)

### entry-002
source: ar.wikipedia — "أرض زيكولا" (verified)
used_in: ch-01
claim: the Zikola school's opening hook = a one-way portal + one hard law + an external deadline
paraphrase: Khaled enters through a forbidden crypt (سرداب فوريك); the door closes behind him and opens only once a year on Zikola Day — the whole plot runs against that clock. The world rule is taught to the newcomer by locals in dialogue (يامن explains the units), not narrator exposition; the newcomer arrives with a personal past-thread (a book tied to his father) that the city immediately makes relevant. The gate scene carries both wonder and dread.

### entry-003
source: en.wikipedia — "Detective fiction" (verified; Golden Age / Knox section)
used_in: ch-01, ch-04
claim: fair-play mystery: all puzzle elements must be present early so the ending gratifies curiosity; reverse chronology is genre-native
paraphrase: Knox's Decalogue (1929) demands the mystery's elements be "clearly presented to the reader at an early stage" — the first-person narrator convention lets the narrator describe the decisive clue without knowing it, so the opening scene should already contain the whole riddle in innocent clothes. "The Three Apples" (1001 Nights) anticipates reverse chronology: open at the aftermath, reconstruct backwards.
quote: a detective story "must have as its main interest the unravelling of a mystery; a mystery whose elements are clearly presented to the reader at an early stage" (Knox, quoted in the article)

## chapter-02

### entry-004
source: ar.wikipedia — "أرض زيكولا" (verified)
used_in: ch-02, ch-03
claim: valuation systems make an abstract currency feel real: public pricing, work that adds units, purchases that subtract; poverty is measurable and visible
paraphrase: every transaction has a unit price; the cost scene lands when the reader watches the price register against a real life. Zikola's yearly festival, where the poorest are chosen by game and slaughtered, is the system's ultimate price — a cost shown, not told. For this book: price memories on a felt scale (how much of "me" is left after the sale); a stranger visibly depleting himself at a counter teaches the rule faster than any speech.
quote: "إن عملة أرض زيكولا هي وحدات الذكاء.. ومن يكون ذكيا هو الغني.. أما الفقير فهو الأقل ذكاء" (quoted on the page)

### entry-005
source: en.wikipedia — "Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind" (verified; Plot / Analysis)
used_in: ch-02, ch-03
claim: the visceral cost of erasure = permanence plus progressive degradation; the loss is felt when the protagonist finds a hole he cannot re-fill
paraphrase: Lacuna erases while the client sleeps and memories degrade gradually rather than vanishing instantly — dramatize the payment as erosion (a memory losing color and detail) so the reader feels it moment by moment; Joel re-experiences memories in reverse order as they die. Irreversibility (no refund) is what makes the first purchase horrifying.

### entry-006
source: en.wikipedia — "Eternal Sunshine..." (verified) + Zikola's Asil arc (verified, same page as entry-004)
used_in: ch-02, ch-05
claim: suspicious-buyer asymmetry: the buyer knows the seller's memory better than the seller does
paraphrase: Patrick re-sells Clementine's erased memories of Joel by using them to seduce her — purchased memories weaponized against the seller. Zikola mirrors the asymmetry when الطبيبة أسيل secretly holds/transfers units and reverses Khaled's fortune. In this book: the buyer who greets the protagonist "like an old friend" is the first crack in the stranger-illusion — he recognizes the protagonist's past because he holds its memories.

## chapter-03

### entry-007
source: en.wikipedia — "Eternal Sunshine..." (verified; Plot)
used_in: ch-03
claim: deleted memories leave records — a paper-trail mechanic
paraphrase: Lacuna keeps files of every erased memory; Mary steals and mails them, so erasure stays traceable after the fact. Joel also hides Clementine inside memories unrelated to her to save her — memories can be concealed inside other memories, which is exactly what makes a "foundational" memory worth stealing: its removal unmoors everything layered on it. Black-market thread: a ledger of paid memories exists somewhere; whoever holds it holds leverage over every seller.

### entry-008
source: The Giver, Lois Lowry (1993) — well-known work, cited from memory
used_in: ch-03, ch-04
claim: foundational-memory stakes: the community erased all memory; Jonas inherits the stored archive and becomes the only "whole" person
paraphrase: forgetting is portrayed as numbness — erasing the foundational memory (color, pain, love) hollows out the memories built on it. In this book, paying a foundational memory should visibly unravel later memories (they blur, contradict, or collapse) — that decay is both the cost and the clue the protagonist misreads until ch-04.

### entry-009
source: en.wikipedia — "Detective fiction" (verified; The Moonstone section) + ar.wikipedia Zikola plot (verified)
used_in: ch-03
claim: mid-story reversal must reuse established rules, not introduce new ones
paraphrase: The Moonstone canonized the twist arsenal (red herrings, least-likely suspect, reconstruction of the crime); Zikola's mid-story flip — Khaled, who believes himself solvent, is seized as one of the poorest after a re-valuation — shows the reversal lands hardest when the protagonist's own earlier payments are re-counted against him. In ch-03: the foundational payment's true price is revealed by the very ledger his payments have been writing.

## chapter-04

### entry-010
source: Shutter Island, D. Lehane (2003; film 2010) — well-known work, cited from memory
used_in: ch-04
claim: "he was never a stranger" reveals re-read the opening scene; they do not add to it
paraphrase: Teddy's investigation is re-read as the hospital's therapeutic role-play; every anomaly of the opening (the odd staff, the extra guard, the fog) re-signifies once the frame flips. For ch-04: the opening's oddities (the gatekeeper's familiarity, the unpayable debt, the memory gaps) must be exactly the details the reveal explains — nothing new should be needed.

### entry-011
source: Memento, C. Nolan (2000) + The Sandman, N. Gaiman — well-known works, cited from memory
used_in: ch-04, ch-05
claim: identity-loops: external memory substitutes and chosen self-deception
paraphrase: Leonard's tattoos and notes are external memory he trusts absolutely — the reader discovers his earlier selves wrote them and they lie; the loop closes when the protagonist chooses to keep the lie. Sandman motif: dreams are woven from memory, so forgetting fragments the dreamer. Applied here: the city re-issuing paid memories to new buyers means the protagonist's erased identity is walking around in someone else's head — the memory-recycling loop is the reveal's engine.

### entry-012
source: en.wikipedia — "Detective fiction" (verified; Knox quote) + TV Tropes "Chekhov's Gun" (verified; Ass Pull)
used_in: ch-04
claim: avoid the convenient flashback: the reveal must be derivable from material already shown
paraphrase: fair-play demands every reveal ingredient be on the page before the reveal (Knox: curiosity gratified because the elements were early); TV Tropes names the failure-mode (Ass Pull) when the payoff detail never appeared. Zikola models the clean version: the father's book is introduced in the first act and pays off at the end. For ch-04: the re-interpretation must re-read ch-01's own sentences, not flashback new facts.

## chapter-05

### entry-013
source: Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (verified ending) + The Giver (well-known)
used_in: ch-05
claim: final-choice structures where the protagonist knowingly re-enters pain
paraphrase: ESOTSM ends with Joel and Clementine choosing to try again after hearing their own bitter tapes — "pay yourself": accept the cost, keep the memory. The Giver ends with Jonas breaking the rule — fleeing with the memories he was paid to hold — into a deliberately unresolved Elsewhere. Both fit "pay yourself or break the rule": the payment is meaningful only if it is the last thing the protagonist can spend.

### entry-014
source: The Usual Suspects (1995) — well-known + TV Tropes "Call-Back"/"Brick Joke" (linked from Chekhov's Gun page, verified)
used_in: ch-05
claim: endings that re-frame the whole book as one transaction
paraphrase: The Usual Suspects' ending rebuilds the entire narrative as Verbal's fabrication from objects in the room — the last page should make the reader re-price the whole story under the new rule. Call-backs (echoing ch-01's breadcrumb line on the final page) prove retroactively that the opening was planted; "the debt was the protagonist himself" works because every payment was installments on the same purchase.

### entry-015
source: The Giver (well-known, ambiguous ending) + Knox via "Detective fiction" (verified)
used_in: ch-05
claim: ambiguity vs resolution: resolve the mechanics, hold the person open
paraphrase: fair-play obliges the riddle (the debt, the recycling, the opening scene) to close — curiosity must be "gratified at the end." The Giver shows the emotional question (is Elsewhere real? does the escape work?) may stay open without damaging the book: it converts mechanics-resolution into theme. For ch-05: the system must close; the protagonist's fate may be the final ambiguity — a deliberate choice, not a loose end.
