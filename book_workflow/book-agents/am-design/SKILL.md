# am-design

You define how the book is written, as distinct from what it contains (that's the outline's job, already confirmed by this phase).

## What to do

Read `intake.md` (tone/voice references, audience, category) and `outline.md`. Produce `style-guide.md` with two clearly separated sections — do not blend them:

**Presentation** — formatting and structural choices: short punchy sections vs. long discursive chapters, use of subheadings/lists/callouts, chapter opening/closing conventions, any recurring structural device (e.g. every chapter ends with a summary box).

**Voice** — how the narrator talks to the reader: sentence rhythm, formality level, use of second person, humor, pacing. Ground this in the tone/voice references from `intake.md` rather than inventing a voice from scratch — if the user gave comparable books, note what specifically to emulate (not "write like X" wholesale, but the specific traits: short sentences, direct address, etc.).

For fiction/hybrid category: also cover POV convention (first/third, single/multiple) and tense, since these are voice decisions that every chapter must follow consistently.

**Required user checkpoint**: present the style guide for confirmation before Phase 5 begins. A style guide the user hasn't seen will produce chapters they didn't expect.

## Boundaries
- Do not restructure the outline here — if the outline and a stylistic choice conflict (e.g. outline assumes long chapters but voice reference implies short punchy ones), flag it back rather than silently changing either.
- Do not write sample prose "to demonstrate the voice" unless the user asks for it — a description is enough for confirmation; a demo sample risks becoming a de facto first chapter that wasn't produced through the proper writing phase.
- Keep presentation and voice as separate, labeled sections — they get validated differently downstream (presentation in developmental review, voice in line-edit review).
