## Established facts / claims (nonfiction)

_Seeded at book start; populated by each chapter's writer agent via append. Do not rewrite prior entries — append-only._

- **Cue-routine architecture** (Wood, W. & Rünger, D., 2016, *Annual Review of Psychology* 67; Lally, P. et al., 2010, *European Journal of Social Psychology* 40(6)): A behavior becomes habitual through repeated execution in the presence of a stable environmental signal (the *cue*). Once formed, the cue-context association is preserved across short lapses — Wood calls this *habit persistence*. The cue does the work; the routine is what runs after the cue fires. This is the load-bearing concept ch-02 (deep-work block), ch-03 (shutdown ritual), ch-04 (weekly review), and ch-05 (repair protocol) all attach to. It is the mechanism behind every later chapter's "attach the new habit to an existing cue" instruction.
- **Habit-formation timeline** (Lally et al., 2010): Habit automaticity takes a *median* of 66 days to reach asymptote, with a 95th-percentile range of 18–254 days. The popular "21 days to form a habit" claim (Maxwell Maltz, 1960) is not empirically supported in this study. The 14-day install window the book is built around is for *runnability*, not automaticity.
- **Structured start > specific duration** (decision-002): The value of a morning routine is the cognitive scaffolding it provides, not the specific duration. The 30-minute-or-less ceiling in ch-01 is a *convention the reader can adjust*, not an evidence-backed optimum. Justification on willpower-depletion / decision-fatigue / ego-depletion grounds is forbidden (Hagger et al. 2016 multi-lab pre-registered replication failed the underlying model).

## Terminology

_Seeded at book start; populated by each chapter's writer agent via append._

- **Structured start** — A morning sequence of small decisions (planning + first-task commitment) that runs before the day's reactive demands arrive. The book's framing for ch-01's morning routine. Emphasizes *structure*, not duration (decision-002).
- **Cue-routine architecture** — The cue-driven design pattern on which the book's system rests. A cue is a stable environmental signal (first coffee, desk, playlist, closing a door); the routine is what runs after the cue fires. See "Established facts" above.
- **Tomorrow card** — The card (or task-tool entry) the reader writes *tomorrow's first task and its cue* on, before doing anything else tomorrow morning. The single concrete artifact ch-01 asks the reader to produce. Term introduced in ch-01. (Equivalent digital form: a single entry in the reader's task tool.)

## Voice rules (quick reference — full detail in style-guide.md)

- Second person ("you"), present tense for instructions, past tense for cited findings.
- Every chapter opens on a scene (concrete moment in the reader's likely day) and closes on a single imperative (the chapter's outcome line, verbatim from outline.md).
- Outcome lines, not summaries, end each chapter — the reader knows what they will do, not just what they learned.
- Citation hygiene: name the source inline (e.g. "Newport argues …", "Lally's 2010 study found …"). No footnote-style hanging claims.
- No "as we will see", no "in the next chapter", no internal cross-references — each chapter must stand on its own (per style-guide.md).
