# Style Guide — مدينة الذكريات

Status: DRAFT — user must confirm this guide before Phase 6 (writing) begins.

## Presentation

- **Language:** Modern Standard Arabic (فصحى) throughout, including dialogue — one register, no dialect shifts (confirm with user).
- **Chapter rhythm:** 5 chapters × ~25 pages (≈5,000–6,000 words each). Each chapter = exactly 3 beats (outline b1–b3), ~1,700–2,000 words per beat. Every beat opens mid-action and ends on a turn; no filler bridging.
- **Section breaks:** centered `* * *` on its own line between beats. The chapter's last beat ends on the hook — no trailing break.
- **Chapter opening (recurring):** cold open — one sensory anchor (sound/smell/object) in the first line, ≤3 sentences before the first dialogue line; no setting inventory, no weather, no throat-clearing. ch-01's opening must read casual and innocent while planting the full puzzle (Knox; outline ch-01 draws on entry-003).
- **Chapter closing (recurring):** every chapter ends on its outline hook. Last 2 lines ≤8 words each; the hook re-contextualizes an item already shown (coin, greeting, ledger line); it introduces nothing new; it flips the power balance (who knows whom, what a payment means).
- **Frozen-line rule:** any sentence destined for the ch-04 re-read (outline ch-04 b1) is frozen verbatim at writing time — ch-01 wording is canonical; ch-04 copies it byte-for-byte with zero edits. Track frozen lines per chapter in the ledger while writing.
- **Recurring devices (allowed):** objects/phrases that resurface via the interlink map (breadcrumbs); ledgers and documents as physical plot objects; the deadline countdown stated ≥1× per chapter from ch-01 onward.
- **Self-critique handoff block:** append the exact `<!-- Self-critique -->` block (Outline coverage / Voice match / Bible consistency / Open questions) at the bottom of every chapter file per book-writer/SKILL.md:61–73. Handoff signal, not prose; strip before publication (CLAUDE.md:74).
- **Dialogue formatting:** each speech on its own line; marks `« »` for speech; interruption with `—` at the break point; a physical beat may replace the tag; tag only when speaker identity is unclear; banned: adverb tags ("قال بغضب").

## Voice

- **Reference (structural only, contradiction-001):** Zikola tone traits — rising mystery, fast dialogue, solvable puzzles, emotional cost per payment, power-flipping chapter ends, interlinked breadcrumbs. No meta or marketing reference to the source work anywhere in prose.
- **Register mix:** conversational MSA core ≈70% (narrator thinks/speaks like an 18–35 reader); formal MSA ≈30%, reserved for institutions — the scribe, the exchanger, the auctioneer. Rules gain weight from register contrast: the rule sentences come from formal mouths, never from the narrator's summary.
- **Cadence:** median sentence ≈9 words (range 5–15). Tension/payment beats: ≤12 words/sentence, 1–3 sentences/paragraph. Erosion scenes: fragments ≤6 words, one sensory detail per fragment, one image per erosion — never metaphor stacking.
- **Breath allowance:** exactly one breathing paragraph in the book — ch-05 b2 (the choice), max 5 sentences, ≤40 words each. Everywhere else, punchy.
- **Erosion rendering (visceral rule):** the erasing memory is described from inside, symptom-first — cold, dimming light around a face, words failing mid-sentence, an empty palm. Show the symptom; ban emotion-naming adjectives (حزن، ألم، خوف) and crying-on-page; the reader supplies the emotion from the symptom.
- **Vocabulary:** literate-25-year-old MSA; abstract nouns only in formal dialogue; in action/erosion lines prefer short concrete nouns and verbs. No typographic emphasis (Arabic has no italics) — emphasis via brevity.
- **Taboo devices (banned):** convenient flashbacks (entry-012 — the past enters only via dialogue/secondhand); info-dumps (the rule is shown through dialogue, outline ch-01 b2 — max 1 exposition sentence per scene, spoken by an institution); narrator omniscience (no interiority of other characters, no unshown facts, no "later I learned"); Ass-Pull reveals (ch-04 imports nothing new — every reveal re-reads what ch-01–03 showed); dream/reset cop-outs; "كما لو" similes in erosion scenes.
- **Humor:** narrow — dry, ironic, self-deprecating; max 1 line per scene; only in low-stakes beats (queue, bargaining, first meal); never within 100 words of a chapter hook; never in erosion/debt scenes. Structural rule: the narrator's jokes about forgetting must read, by ch-04, as confessions he couldn't hear himself make.

## POV & tense

- **POV:** first person, single narrator — the "I" of the debt. No POV switches across the 5 chapters.
- **Tense: present (المضارع).** Justification tied to the reveal structure: (1) outline ch-01 already marks "متكلم أول حاضرة"; (2) erosion is felt moment-by-moment from inside (ch-02 b2) — present makes the decay live and un-rewindable, matching the one-way door; (3) ch-04 re-reads ch-01 lines verbatim — present lets one sentence flip meaning with no tense machinery; (4) past tense implies a survivor narrating from after the story, which undercuts the erasure stakes and the deliberately open ending (ch-05 b3).
- **Consistency rules:** one tense everywhere, including remembered content — render as present fragments, never past-perfect; dialogue tags stay present (يقول، يأخذ); the deadline stays present (متبقٍ يوم).
- **Fair-play misreading (Knox):** three tests every misread must pass —
  1. *Anchored*: the misread lives in a sentence that survives verbatim for the ch-04 re-read.
  2. *Seeded*: at the moment of misreading, the true reading is derivable from words already on the page + prior breadcrumbs.
  3. *Reasonable*: the misread is what a sane person would conclude (mistaken identity, exhaustion, coincidence) — the reader may be fooled, never cheated.
  When the narrator misreads, show the alternate cue (the guard's pause, the coin's weight) on-page in the same scene.

## Examples (before/after cadence)

**A. Erosion — present, fragments, symptom-first**
Before: «دفعتُ ثمن الوجبة، وشعرتُ بحزنٍ غامض، وأدركتُ أنني فقدتُ شيئًا ثمينًا، وكان الحذف مؤلمًا، فنسيتُه بعدها.» (past; named emotions; summary)
After: «يأخذ الصرّاف العملة. لا شيء. ثم تنطفئ لمبة في رأسي. أرى وجهًا، يبدأ الضوء حوله ينكمش. أفتح فمي لأسمّي صاحب الوجه… فلا أجد الاسم. لا أبكي. أُنسى.» (present; symptom-only; fragments)

**B. Rule through dialogue, not info-dump (ch-01 b2)**
Before: «كانت القاعدة في هذه المدينة أن كل شراءٍ يحذف ذكرى حقيقية، وتقيّم الصرافة الذكريات نقديًا وفق نظامٍ دقيق لا يعرفه الغرباء.» (narration explains)
After: ««كم الثمن؟» «ستة.» «بأي عملة؟» «بما تنساه.» أضحك: ليس لديّ ما أنساه. لا يضحك البائع. يحدّق في جبهتي كأنها قائمة ثمن: «الجميع يملك شيئًا. حتى أنت.»» (rule lands through exchange; subtext)

**C. Fair-play misread (ch-01 b1 → ch-04 b1)**
Before: «لم أكن أعرف أن الحارس يعرفني من زيارةٍ سابقة؛ فقد دخلتُ المدينة من قبلُ ودفعتُ هويتي ثمنًا للهروب.» (narrator already knows — kills the puzzle)
After: ««أهلًا بعودتك.» يرحّب بي كما يرحّب بمسافرٍ يشبهني. النادل الذي يخلط الوجوه — هذا ما أظنه. أقول: أنت تخلطني بغيري. لا يجادل. يُخرج من جيبه العملة التي لا أعرف مصدرها، ويضعها في كفّي.» (plausible misread; alternate cue = the coin, on-page)
